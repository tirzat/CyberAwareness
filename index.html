CYBER AWARENESS

What Is Cybercrime?

Cybercrime refers to illegal activities carried out using computers, smartphones, networks or the internet. Criminals may use technology to steal money, access private information, damage systems or harass people.

Why Should We Be Aware?

Anyone who uses the internet can become a target. Understanding cyber threats helps individuals, institutions and communities stay safe online.

UNDERSTAND CYBERCRIME

Who Is Behind Cybercrime?

Cybercrime can be committed by individuals or organised groups using digital technology to harm, deceive or exploit others.

🕵️

Individual Cybercriminals

Individuals who use technology to steal information, commit fraud or access accounts without permission.

👥

Organised Criminal Groups

Groups that coordinate online fraud, ransomware and financial scams.

🎭

Online Impersonators

People who create fake identities to deceive or manipulate others.

⚠️

Insider Threats

People who misuse authorised access to confidential information or systems.

Who Can Become a Victim?

🎓

Students

Phishing, cyberbullying and fake profiles.

👴

Senior Citizens

Financial scams and fraudulent calls.

👨‍👩‍👧

Families

Identity theft and online impersonation.

🏢

Businesses

Data theft, malware and ransomware.

Effects of Cybercrime

💰

Financial Loss

Money can be stolen through online fraud and unauthorised transactions.

🧠

Psychological Impact

Victims may experience fear, anxiety and emotional distress.

🏢

Institutional Damage

Organisations may face data breaches, financial losses and service disruption.

🌍

Social Impact

Cybercrime can reduce public trust and affect community safety.

Indian Cyber Laws & Legal Protection

India has laws to address cybercrime, protect digital information and provide legal remedies to victims.

⚖️

Information Technology Act, 2000

Provides legal provisions addressing unauthorised access, identity theft, online cheating and other cyber offences.

🏛️

Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023

Provides criminal law provisions that may apply to cheating, fraud, intimidation and other offences committed using digital technology.

🛡️

Protection of Children

POCSO Act, 2012 provides protection against sexual offences involving children, including relevant online exploitation.

Victim action: Preserve screenshots, messages, transaction IDs and other evidence. Report the incident to the appropriate authorities.

For official legal information, visit India Code.
